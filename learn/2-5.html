2.5 Intervals: The Distance Between Two Notes

Every melody is a chain of intervals. Learn a few by the tunes that start with them.

▶ Open it on the circle

Why it works

An interval is counted in half-steps (semitones). Small intervals (2nds) sound like steps; 3rds sound sweet; perfect 4ths, 5ths and octaves sound open because their frequencies form simple ratios (4:3, 3:2, 2:1); the tritone (6 half-steps) and 7ths sound tense and want to move. Memory hooks use public-domain tunes: Frère Jacques (major 2nd), When the Saints (major 3rd), Wagner’s Bridal Chorus (perfect 4th), Twinkle Twinkle (perfect 5th), My Bonnie (major 6th).

Compare

  • Steps & thirds: Major 2nd, major 3rd, minor 3rd, minor 2nd: small, singable distances.
  • Perfect & wide: Perfect 4th, perfect 5th, octave, major 6th: open, strong leaps.
  • Tense: Tritone, minor 7th, major 7th, minor 6th: intervals that want to move.

Try it

Switch between the three sets. On each beat the low note is C; listen to how far the second note travels.
