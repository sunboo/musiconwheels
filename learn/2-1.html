2.1 The Color of Sound (Rainbow & Octaves)

The 12 chromatic notes follow the visible rainbow from Red (C) to Magenta (B), while octaves shift luminance.

▶ Open it on the circle

Why it works

Doubling a sound wave’s frequency (e.g., C3 at 130.8 Hz to C4 at 261.6 Hz) produces the same note name one octave higher—which is why octaves share the exact same rainbow hue while rising in brightness. Linking colour to pitch is an old idea: Isaac Newton matched his seven rainbow colours to the seven notes of a scale (Opticks, 1704), and the composer Alexander Scriabin wrote a part for a “colour organ” in Prometheus (1910). Concentric’s colours are a learning aid, not a law of nature: try the circle-of-fifths palette in Settings.
