How to read the circle

Around the circle = time. The numbers are the beats of one loop. The needle sweeps clockwise; drag the arc at the top right or tap TAP to set the tempo.
Outside → inside = role. The outer ring is usually the bass, middle rings the chords, the inner ring the melody. Each ring's role is shown in the Layers panel.
Colour = note, brightness = octave. Higher octaves are brighter. Turn on note letters in Settings if colours are hard to tell apart.
Size = loudness. Halo = chord. Big dots are accents, small dots are ghost notes. A halo means a chord (two rings for 7ths, dashed for sus, diamond for diminished). An arc along the ring shows how long a note lasts.
Beat, step, bar. A beat is one pulse of the loop: the numbers around the edge. A step is one slot on a ring: a ring with 8 steps over 4 beats plays two per beat (eighth notes). A bar (measure) is a group of beats; a time signature such as 3/4 means 3 beats per bar. In Indian music the cycle is a taal and its beats are matras: X marks sam (beat 1), 0 marks khali (the wave).
Editing. Press Edit (or click the wheel) to make your own version. Click a dot to add or remove it; long-press or right-click to change its note. Hold "Hold for original" to compare.

Settings

Quality
Lite is smoother on older phones: simpler visuals, no reverb.
Colours
Circle of fifths makes notes that sound good together look alike.
Note letters on dots
Helpful if colours are hard to tell apart.
Vibration
Small buzz when you edit (phones that support it).
See the beat
Flashes the wheel’s edge on every beat (bright on beat 1). For Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, or noisy rooms.
Headphone sync
If the needle runs ahead of the sound (common with Bluetooth), slide right until they line up.
